Overview

Humanoid loco-manipulation requires coordinated locomotion, balance regulation, and manipulation under frequent contacts. FORGE introduces a coarse-to-fine command space spanning base velocity, planar foot placement, wrist-pose tracking, and optional desired wrist-load inputs. A frozen locomotion prior and asymmetric residual actions stabilize whole-body corrections. For sustained contact, a momentum observer is combined with a simulator-supervised learned correction to estimate low-frequency, wrist-equivalent interaction forces from proprioception and reconstructed arm PD torques. A quasi-static virtual-target loop uses this estimate to adapt wrist targets. The force pathway is deliberately scoped to sensorless estimation and contact-load adaptation—not full-wrench sensing, dynamic impedance, or force-setpoint tracking.

Method

Multi-granularity commands, sensorless force estimation, and asymmetric residual control work together through a single task-space interface.

Multi-granularity commands

Navigation, stance, bilateral wrist-pose, and force groups can be activated individually or jointly. Observation and reward masking reduces interference between inactive objectives.

Physics-informed force estimation

An arm-only momentum observer provides a physical prior. A compact learned correction absorbs actuator mismatch and unmodeled whole-body coupling using only deployable signals.

Asymmetric residual control

A frozen lower-body locomotion expert anchors walking, while learned lower-body residuals and direct upper-body actions preserve task-dependent whole-body coordination.

Actor architecture

Six observable frames are organized by command granularity and encoded through shared MLPs before temporal aggregation. Training-only privileged signals supervise adaptation; deployment uses observable proprioception and command histories.

Experiments

Controlled ablations explain the design. Independent force references and hardware task sequences test its behavior.

Control ablations

The locomotion prior prevents the collapse seen in end-to-end 29-DoF learning; residual-action exploration limits preserve the prior, and masking reduces walk-to-stance discontinuities.

Force estimation

On representative staged-load traces, the hybrid estimator yields lower RMSE and MAE than observer-only and learned-only variants.

Hardware evaluation

Representative hardware traces span walking, squat-and-lift, explicit foot positioning, wrist pushes, and base-drag perturbations.

Downstream policy example

A Diffusion Policy trained on 320 demonstrations is used as a downstream example of the task-space interface. Over 20 hardware trials per payload, success rates were 100% for empty and 0.5 kg payloads and 90% for 1 kg and 2 kg payloads. These results are limited to the evaluated payload conditions.
